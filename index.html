About

What this is

A walkable 3D model of Calleva Atrebatum, the Roman town at Silchester. You can collect 3D scans of actual artefacts and dig sites, and talk with ghosts from different periods of Silchester’s history. The aim was to give a sense of what the place might have been like, in an interactive way.

This was a quick project made mainly using AI coding models with about 1 day’s worth of effort, drawing on freely available archeological data. For the historical accuracy see below.

It should work on most modern web browsers and mobile devices.

The structures

The roads, building footprints, earthworks and the ground contours are taken from the GIS scanning data of the Silchester Mapping Project 2005–10.

Since these are based on real mapping data, the positions are true to life, though the building footprints don’t represent the full range of structures that have been present in the town over it’s 500-year inhabited period. They likely represent the most long-lasting Roman buildings.

The buildings themselves are just extruded from the footprints and can only give a vague sense of what the real ones were like.

Several key buildings: the forum, baths, mansio, temples, church and amphitheatre are rough 3D reconstructions and are not exact archeological models.

Collectable 3D scans

These are 3D models linked directly to their source in Reading University and Reading Museum-linked websites. There are more of these that can be added, and I suspect many people are not aware they are available.

Ghosts

You can find and talk to a number of ghosts from throughout the site’s key history. Each ghost only knows their own time, for example the Iron Age Atrebates farmer will not talk about the Roman forum or the stone walls, as these would not be built until hundreds of years later.

The ghost dialogue is all pre-scripted and appropriate for children say over 9 and over. It was generated and checked using the Gemini AI model so may not be 100% accurate, but is hopefully evocative. There is no live ‘AI chat’ access.
