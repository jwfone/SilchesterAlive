# labourer walks

### Walk 1

**Ghost:** Greetings to you. Step into the yards out of the drizzle. I was just stacking fresh roof tiles for the kiln before the rain ruins the lot. Care to see how we work?
**You:** What is this big wall you are building?
**Ghost:** This great wall of stone will stretch all the way around our town. It is a hard task for us builders, hauling materials and mixing mortar day after day, but it will keep Calleva safe for generations to come.
**You:** How do the seasons change your job?
**Ghost:** Winter frost is our greatest enemy because it cracks the wet mortar and ruins our hard work. When the heavy rains come, the lime washes right out of the wall joints, so we must stop building. On wet days, we huddle in the sheds and mould clay tiles instead.
**You:** What do you put on when you go to work?
**Ghost:** Heavy coarse wool tunic keeps off the chill when I am laying flint. A stout leather belt holds my tunic close so it does not snag on scaffold poles. On my feet, thick nailed leather boots protect me from dropped stones and wet lime mortar every single day.
**You:** Tell me about your home.
**Ghost:** Living in a work hut by the yard means I am never far from my tools. My mates and I share these rough shelters, keeping a small fire going to dry our damp woollen clothes when the weather turns.
**You:** What do you do from morning to night?
**Ghost:** Sunrise always finds us ready by the building yards, waiting for the master builder's orders. We spend the long hours lifting flint blocks and spreading wet mortar under the watchful eyes of the foremen. When darkness falls, we finally drop our iron tools and seek our warm hearths.
**You:** What do you do at the end of the day?
**Ghost:** May the weather stay fine for your journey today. Goodbye!

### Walk 2

**Ghost:** Back again? Excellent. The mortar is fresh and the gang is hard at work on the eastern stretch. Sit by the timber piles if you want to rest your legs a bit.
**You:** Tell me about your food.
**Ghost:** We eat basic fare here in the yards, mostly bread and thick vegetable stew. The cheese is hard enough to chip a tooth, but it fills the belly. We cook together over the yard fires after dusk.
**You:** What animals do you see working in the yards?
**Ghost:** Huge oxen teamed in pairs drag carts stacked high with grey flint from local pits. Without their brute strength, we could never move enough stone to build this wall. Smaller donkeys carry sacks of sand, and guard dogs keep watch over the yards at night.
**You:** What is in your tool bag?
**Ghost:** I rely on my iron trowel, wooden moulds, and heavy hammers to get the job done. The trowel is for spreading the lime mortar smoothly, while the heavy iron hammer helps me trim the rough edges of the flint stones so they fit snugly in the wall.
**You:** What is the wall made of?
**Ghost:** Grey flint and white mortar are the secrets to our town wall. We dig the flints from the fields nearby and burn limestone to make the lime. When they join together, they turn into a solid mass of stone that will stand against the wind and rain for generations.
**You:** Where do you get all the stones?
**Ghost:** Farming folk dig the flints out of the fields around Calleva and bring them to us in ox carts. The stones are a nuisance to the farmers, so they are glad to sell them to the town. We sort them by size, saving the biggest ones for the outer layers.
**You:** Is the mortar hard to make?
**Ghost:** May the weather stay fine for your journey today. Goodbye!

**Ghost:** Huge oxen teamed in pairs drag carts stacked high with grey flint from local pits. Without their brute strength, we could never move enough stone to build this wall. Smaller donkeys carry sacks of sand, and guard dogs keep watch over the yards at night.
**You:** What tools do you use for your work?
**Ghost:** I rely on my iron trowel, wooden moulds, and heavy hammers to get the job done. The trowel is for spreading the lime mortar smoothly, while the heavy iron hammer helps me trim the rough edges of the flint stones so they fit snugly in the wall.
**You:** Who are you and what is your job?
**Ghost:** I am Duro, a simple builder working on Calleva's grand stone wall. When I am not laying heavy stone and flint, I am busy shaping and firing red clay roof tiles in my kiln by the building yards.
**You:** Tell me about the wall around the town.
**Ghost:** May the weather stay fine for your journey today. Goodbye!

**You:** Who pays you your money?

**You:** What can you buy in the town?
**Ghost:** I spend my wages on filling my belly and keeping a dry roof over my head. Occasionally, I buy a fresh pair of thick leather soles from the town shoemaker to repair my boots. The rest of my money goes on small beer to share with my working mates.
**You:** What news have you heard from the traders?
**Ghost:** Folk passing through the gates bring wild talk from the eastern roads. They say there is a new emperor again, but we just keep laying our flint. As long as the coins have a face on them, we do not worry about who wears the purple.
**You:** Do you ever watch any shows?
**Ghost:** Mind your footing on the wet gravel. Fare you well.

### Walk 8

**Ghost:** Welcome back to Calleva. Rain delayed us this morning, but the sun is breaking through now. What shall we chat about while the lime mortar dries?

**Ghost:** Just beyond our new stone wall, to the east, lies the great amphitheatre. It has high banks to keep the crowds safe. We get all sorts of entertainment there, from traveling players to contests of strength, which makes a grand change from lifting heavy flints all day.
**You:** What is this big wall you are building?
**Ghost:** This great wall of stone will stretch all the way around our town. It is a hard task for us builders, hauling materials and mixing mortar day after day, but it will keep Calleva safe for generations to come.
**You:** Is the wall going to be very tall?
**Ghost:** We aim to build it high enough that no climber could easily scale it. Right now, it is up to my chest in some parts, but the scaffolding goes higher every week. It is a truly grand sight from the yards.
**You:** What is the wall made of?
**Ghost:** We set local flint stones into thick layers of wet lime mortar to build the core. The flints are incredibly hard to break, but they have sharp edges that hold onto the mortar beautifully. It makes a wall so tough that even the heaviest iron rams could not shake it.
**You:** Is the mortar hard to make?
**Ghost:** The mortar is a simple mix of burnt limestone, sand, and clean water. We stir it until it is thick and creamy, like a heavy porridge. My mates and I take turns at the mixing troughs because it quickly tires out your shoulders and back.
**You:** Who brings the stones to the wall?
**Ghost:** Off you go then. I must get back to my kilns.

**Ghost:** Pouring a little beer on the stone base of a shrine is our common way to pray. I hold up my hands, ask for strong knees and safe scaffolding, then leave a tiny copper piece. The gods like a sincere heart more than grand gold, folk say.
**You:** Do you have friends in the building yard?
**Ghost:** My mates are a good-humoured bunch of builders, always ready with a joke to pass the long hours. We share our simple meals of bread and cheese, sitting on piles of timber in the yard. You need good friends when you are lifting heavy stone in the biting wind.
**You:** Where do you live?
**Ghost:** May the weather stay fine for your journey today. Goodbye!

### Walk 20

**Ghost:** Back again? Excellent. The mortar is fresh and the gang is hard at work on the eastern stretch. Sit by the timber piles if you want to rest your legs a bit.
**You:** Why is there a great stone wall here?
**Ghost:** Calleva's new wall is rising fast, replacing the old earth banks. My gang and I are busy laying heavy flint stones and bonding tiles to make it strong. It will protect our homes and show everyone our town's strength.
**You:** Does the weather affect your work?
**Ghost:** Winter frost is our greatest enemy because it cracks the wet mortar and ruins our hard work. When the heavy rains come, the lime washes right out of the wall joints, so we must stop building. On wet days, we huddle in the sheds and mould clay tiles instead.
**You:** How do you make the wall so strong?
**Ghost:** We set local flint stones into thick layers of wet lime mortar to build the core. The flints are incredibly hard to break, but they have sharp edges that hold onto the mortar beautifully. It makes a wall so tough that even the heaviest iron rams could not shake it.
**You:** Where do you get all the stones?
**Ghost:** We get the stones from pits nearby and from the fields where crops grow. Oxen haul them in heavy wooden wagons right to the foot of the wall. My mates and I then carry them up the wooden scaffolding in wicker baskets on our backs.
**You:** What is in your tool bag?
**Ghost:** I rely on my iron trowel, wooden moulds, and heavy hammers to get the job done. The trowel is for spreading the lime mortar smoothly, while the heavy iron hammer helps me trim the rough edges of the flint stones so they fit snugly in the wall.
**You:** How are your tile moulds made?
**Ghost:** Off you go then. I must get back to my kilns.
