# traveller walks

### Walk 1

**Ghost:** Good day to you! I have just arrived through the city gates with sweet olive oil and rich red vintage. Come, sit a moment and share the local news with me.
**You:** Tell me about the wine you sell.
**Ghost:** The red wine in my jars was harvested in sunny vineyards across the sea. I carry both vintage wines for wealthy households and younger, sharper wines for common taverns. Every drops stays sweet inside sealed clay amphorae.
**You:** Is the market very busy?
**Ghost:** Market day is a wonderfully noisy and busy time here in the great forum. Local farmers bring in fresh vegetables, fine woollen cloaks, and earthenware pottery to sell. I set up my own stand to trade my southern wine and oil for British goods.
**You:** What do you drink with your meals?
**Ghost:** I eat hearty local pottage and spelt bread at the inn, washed down with my own diluted wine. The innkeeper makes a thick stew with local peas and salted pork that really warms the bones. It is simple fare, but very welcome after a long journey.
**You:** Is it very cold in your inn room?
**Ghost:** Cold winds howl through the courtyard when winter arrives, making my room quite drafty. I pay the innkeeper extra for a steady supply of dry wood and charcoal for my brazier. Without that glowing fire and my heavy blankets, I would freeze indeed.
**You:** Is the weather here very bad?
**Ghost:** This wet climate is far colder and muddier than my sunny home in Gaul. The downpours can wash away the edges of the paths, leaving us stranded in the countryside. I must wear my thickest woollen cloak just to keep the dampness from freezing my bones.
**You:** Do your mules hate the wet weather?
**Ghost:** Go carefully beyond the town gates, my friend.

### Walk 2

**Ghost:** You return! Excellent. My horse is fed, the stables are quiet, and I am still resting here at the mansio. Have you come to talk of trade and travel again?
**You:** What is this town like?
**Ghost:** Imagine a proud town encircled by thick stone walls and guarded by four impressive gates. It is a bit quieter now than it used to be, but the streets are still full of life. It is a grand place to trade wine and secure fine British hides.
**You:** Where do Christians go to pray here?
**Ghost:** Passing by the forum, you can spot the small church house. I often see folk entering its quiet doors in the morning, holding simple lamps before heading out to tend their fields or shops.
**You:** What do you do every day?
**Ghost:** Early mornings begin with feeding my heavy draft animals and checking my wagons. I then walk into the bustling market square, where I meet buyers and arrange trades for British grain. Nights are spent sharing stories with other travellers over dinner.
**You:** Where do travellers sleep in Calleva?
**Ghost:** My home in Calleva is a cosy room at the mansio courtyard inn. It is a grand building meant for travellers on official business. There is plenty of space for my wagons, and the kitchen serves hot pottage every evening.
**You:** What do you eat at the inn?
**Ghost:** The innkeeper serves simple, filling meals to us travellers. We usually get thick pottage made with peas and barley, crisp spelt bread, and salted pork. Naturally, I prefer to drink a little of my own wine with dinner.
**You:** What can you see in the courtyard?
**Ghost:** Safe journeys to you until we meet again.

**Ghost:** We spend many evenings at the inn checking our wheels and tightening the iron fittings. The rough paved roads of Britain wear down the timber quickly, so we must grease the axles regularly to prevent them from catching fire from the friction.
**You:** Tell me about your amphorae jars.
**Ghost:** We use these traditional clay amphorae to transport both rich olive oil and fine southern wine. They have two sturdy handles near the neck so we can lift and carry them, though it takes two strong men to move a full one.
**You:** What do you drink with your meals?
**Ghost:** May the roads treat you kindly today. Farewell!

**Ghost:** You return! Excellent. My horse is fed, the stables are quiet, and I am still resting here at the mansio. Have you come to talk of trade and travel again?
**You:** Tell me about yourself and your work.
**Ghost:** Leontius at your service. My business is trade, travelling the Roman roads with my heavy wagons. I import sweet southern wine and olive oil for the townsfolk here, then fill my carts with fine British wool, grain, and animal hides before returning south.
**You:** What do you buy to take back to Gaul?
**Ghost:** Once my wine jars are emptied, I fill my empty wagons with rich British grain and tough cattle hides. The soil here grows wonderful crops, and folk back home in Gaul will pay good money for such fine produce.
**You:** How do you keep the grain dry on the journey?
**Ghost:** Protecting the golden grain is my top priority on the road. We use tightly bound sacks made of thick hemp, which we cover with heavy coverings. If even a little water gets inside, the wheat will spoil and mold before we ever reach the coast, so my drivers inspect the wagons constantly.
**You:** How do you agree on prices in the town?
**Ghost:** Coins are far harder to come by these days, my friends. I must bargain sharply over every single bronze piece to get a fair price for my oil. Sometimes we even swap wine directly for local wool or grain when silver is scarce.
**You:** Where do you get your wine and oil?
**Ghost:** I bring rich wine pressed from sun-ripened grapes in Gaul, along with clear olive oil. Townsfolk buy it eagerly, as southern vintages are much sweeter than local ale. I store it in strong amphorae to keep it fresh.
**You:** What is market day like in Calleva?
**Ghost:** May the roads treat you kindly today. Farewell!
