# swineherd walks

### Walk 1

**Ghost:** Mind your step on the overgrown path. I am Wulfhere, a simple swineherd. I keep my beasts well away from the deep, dark wells of the old ones. What brings you to these quiet ruins?
**You:** Who are you and what do you do here?
**Ghost:** People hereabouts know me as Wulfhere, the swineherd who wanders the ancient, quiet spaces of Silchester with his animals. I guide my clever pigs to the best feeding spots among the fallen walls. It is a peaceful life, so long as the wolves keep their distance.
**You:** What kind of home do you have here?
**Ghost:** My home is a humble timber-and-thatch hut dug into the earth, safely placed outside the spooky stone walls. We dug it deep to keep out the biting winter winds that sweep across the open fields. It is cozy inside when the hearth fire is burning bright.
**You:** Where do you cook?
**Ghost:** Inside our timber home, the firepit sits right in the middle of the dirt floor. We hang an iron pot from a wooden tripod to boil our barley. The grey smoke drifts up and slowly finds its way out through the thick straw thatch of our roof.
**You:** What do you do when it snows?
**Ghost:** Winter winds howl fiercely across the old ruins, so we block our door with thick hides. We pile dry straw high around our sleeping mats to keep the chill away. Our hearth fire is kept burning day and night to warm our bones.
**You:** What is the weather like here?
**Ghost:** The changing seasons govern everything we do from dawn until the stars appear. Summer days are long and warm, allowing us to wander far down the straight roads with our herd. When the frost bites our fingers, we stay close to home and pray for an early spring.
**You:** What do your animals eat in the spring?
**Ghost:** Farewell, then. Keep clear of the walls when night falls.

### Walk 2

**Ghost:** You return to Calleva, then? It is good to see a friendly face. My herd has been restless today, sniffing at the old north gate. Come, sit by my hearth fire outside the walls.
**You:** What are your animals like?
**Ghost:** I keep a herd of dark, bristly swine that are very clever at finding their own food in the forest. They are strong, fast beasts that can run quickly if a wolf gets too close to the clearing. Caring for them is mostly about finding the best oak groves.
**You:** Do wolves attack your pigs?
**Ghost:** Protecting my swine from the gray wolves is my constant task. My clever dog barks to warn me when they are near, and we drive the herd back to the safety of our pens before dark.
**You:** Do you have other animals besides pigs?
**Ghost:** Our goats are excellent companions to the pigs, as they eat the weeds the swine leave behind. We keep them penned near our sunken hut at night to protect them from wolves. Their thick coats also give us coarse hair for weaving.
**You:** How do you keep goats safe from wolves?
**Ghost:** We pen them right outside our timber door before the sun drops behind the trees. The high fence of woven stakes stops prowling beasts from creeping near. Having them sleep close by lets us hear if anything disturbs them, so my iron spear is always ready at hand.
**You:** How do you make cheese from goat milk?
**Ghost:** Fresh milk sits in earthen jars until it sours into thick curds. My kin press out the watery whey using woven linen cloths, then sprinkle sea salt to keep it sweet. The small, white cheeses sit on dry wooden shelves inside our cool hut until they are ready to fill our wooden bowls.
**You:** What kind of food do you eat?
**Ghost:** Farewell, then. Keep clear of the walls when night falls.

**Ghost:** Heavy iron vessels are rare, so we rely mostly on simple clay jars placed near the coals. They cook our meals slowly over dry oak logs. We scrub them clean using sand from the stream and dry grass after every evening meal around the hearth.
**You:** How do you deal with all the smoke?
**Ghost:** Dry oak branches yield clean heat and less smoke than green wood gathered from wet ditch banks. The grey plume rises naturally into the steep roof thatch, seeping through the straw without needing a stone chimney. It stings the eyes a bit, but it keeps insects away.
**You:** Do you have a family here?
**Ghost:** We are a small household of kin living just outside the silent, empty walls of the old town. My spouse prepares our simple meals and keeps our woollen clothing mended with bone needles. Our young ones are already learning how to watch the pigs and call them home.

**Ghost:** Farewell, then. Keep clear of the walls when night falls.

### Walk 4

**You:** What are those large stone walls around here?
**Ghost:** The old stone walls are falling down in places, but they still stand tall against the wild forests. Folk say the spirits of the past still wander the quiet spaces inside the circle. I always make sure to be outside before the evening shadows grow long.
**You:** Who built these giant walls?
**Ghost:** Long before our kings ruled, strange folk crafted this vast place with stone and mortar. Now, the busy markets are gone and only my goats climb the grassy mounds inside. It is a peaceful, empty shell of a town, left to the wind and the wild birds.
**You:** Why did everyone leave the old town?
**Ghost:** No man living knows for certain why the old city fell quiet and grey. Some say trade dried up and the markets closed, so folk moved out to farm the fertile valleys. Once the wooden roofs rotted away, only the cold stone shell was left standing in the wild.
**You:** What do old stories say about the old people?
**Ghost:** Old tales say men of great stature raised these giant stones using ancient skills lost to us now. They must have been strong as oaks to lift such huge grey blocks so high. Nobody knows where those mysterious folk went, only that they left their empty stone shells behind.
**You:** Who rules this land?
**Ghost:** Go safely on the old roads. May the sun light your path.

### Walk 5

**Ghost:** You return to Calleva, then? It is good to see a friendly face. My herd has been restless today, sniffing at the old north gate. Come, sit by my hearth fire outside the walls.
**You:** What are your animals like?
**Ghost:** These pigs of mine are hardy, long-legged creatures that look a bit like the wild boars of the deep woods. They spend all day snuffling through the leaf litter for nuts, grubs, and fallen apples. I must watch them closely so they do not wander off.
**You:** What tools do you use?
**Ghost:** My trusty wooden staff keeps the clever pigs from wandering too deep into the dark forest. I keep a small, sharp blade of iron tucked into my belt for my daily chores. When a wolf prowls too close, my horn alerts the neighbors across the valley.
**You:** What is your horn made of?
**Ghost:** An iron blade is precious, so I keep mine greased with tallow to stop rust. I use it to trim hazel pegs or mend leather straps, while the horn hangs nearby to blast a warning if a dog spots a wolf.
**You:** Why do you use ash wood for your stick?
**Ghost:** A proper staff must be tall enough to rest your chin on and tough enough to turn a stray sow. I shaped mine from a seasoned ash branch, rubbing fat into the grain so damp river mist would not rot it.
**You:** How do you buy things?
**Ghost:** Gold coins are of no use to a swineherd. Instead, we swap a fat piglet or a tub of salted lard when we need sharp iron hooks, winter grain, or salt from the coast. It is a simple way of living, but it keeps our bellies full.
**You:** How do travelers get to this place?
**Ghost:** Go safely on the old roads. May the sun light your path.

**You:** How do you protect young piglets?
**Ghost:** Tiny piglets stay inside a sheltered wicker pen right beside our hut until they grow strong enough to walk far. We lay fresh dry straw inside to keep them warm and away from prowling woodland foxes.
**You:** What do your pigs like to eat most?
**Ghost:** Sweet acorns make up their best autumn feast under the canopy. For the rest of the year, I lead them to damp hollows where they can root for juicy bulbs, tender shoots, and fungi hidden under the leaves.
**You:** What tools do you use?
**Ghost:** A sturdy ash staff is my main tool, useful for guiding the stubborn swine and keeping them in line. I also wear a sharp iron knife at my waist for cutting thick rope or slicing wood. At my side hangs a hollow horn to sound a loud warning if danger comes.
**You:** How do you buy things?
**Ghost:** Go safely on the old roads. May the sun light your path.

**Ghost:** Still wandering these old roads, traveller? I am glad the spirits of the empty town did not snatch you away. My pigs are grazing safely, so I have time for a brief chat.
**You:** What is for dinner in your hut?
**Ghost:** Barley porridge and flatbread make up most of our meals, cooked right over our central hearth fire. We also have tangy cheese from our few goats and whatever sweet berries we find in the summer hedges. Meat is a rare treat, saved for the coldest days of the year.
**You:** How do you care for your herd of swine?
**Ghost:** I keep a herd of dark, bristly swine that are very clever at finding their own food in the forest. They are strong, fast beasts that can run quickly if a wolf gets too close to the clearing. Caring for them is mostly about finding the best oak groves.
**You:** Do wolves attack your pigs?
**Ghost:** Wild beasts roam the forest around the empty town. Wolves are the main danger, especially in the winter months when food is scarce. I carry a strong staff to drive them away.
**You:** How do you watch your pigs?
**Ghost:** I carry a long staff of ash wood to drive the herd through the thickets and brambles. A good iron knife helps me chop stakes for our pens or clean a goat's hoof. To call my family or scare away pests, I blow a loud, clear note on my horn.
**You:** How did you make your wooden staff?
**Ghost:** Wood from the ash tree serves a swineherd best because it stays light in the hand yet hits hard against bad briars. I carved the top smooth to fit my palm comfortably during long days of walking.
**You:** Where do you get your clothes?
**Ghost:** Farewell, then. Keep clear of the walls when night falls.

### Walk 20

**Ghost:** You return to Calleva, then? It is good to see a friendly face. My herd has been restless today, sniffing at the old north gate. Come, sit by my hearth fire outside the walls.
**You:** What kind of home do you have here?
**Ghost:** I live in a small, sunken timber hut just beyond the old stone walls of this quiet town. We built it with thick wooden posts and a roof of dry straw thatch to keep the rain away. It is much warmer down in the ground than living on top of it.
**You:** How do you cook your food?
**Ghost:** Inside our timber home, the firepit sits right in the middle of the dirt floor. We hang an iron pot from a wooden tripod to boil our barley. The grey smoke drifts up and slowly finds its way out through the thick straw thatch of our roof.
**You:** How do you deal with all the smoke?
**Ghost:** Thick thatch high above our heads absorbs the drifting smoke from our central fire pit. While the air inside can grow hazy on rainy days, the smoke serves a good purpose by keeping flies out of our home. We simply sit low on low wooden stools.
**You:** What kind of food do you eat?
**Ghost:** Barley porridge and flatbread make up most of our meals, cooked right over our central hearth fire. We also have tangy cheese from our few goats and whatever sweet berries we find in the summer hedges. Meat is a rare treat, saved for the coldest days of the year.
**You:** What are your animals like?
**Ghost:** My swine are clever, hairy beasts that love to forage for sweet acorns under the great oak trees. They have thick, dark bristles to protect them from the thorny brambles and sharp tusks to dig up tasty roots. They know my voice and follow me easily.
**You:** Do you have other animals besides pigs?
**Ghost:** Go safely on the old roads. May the sun light your path.
