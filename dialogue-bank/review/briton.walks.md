# briton walks

### Walk 1

**Ghost:** Greetings, friend. Step closer to the hearth fire if you are cold. The damp gets into the bones this time of year. What news do you bring to our roundhouse?
**You:** What is your home like?
**Ghost:** We dwell in a round house built from sturdy oak posts and woven branches. The clay walls stop winter winds, while a central hearth fills the space with light and heat. It is a comfortable dwelling for my kin, warm even in damp weather.
**You:** How do the dykes protect you?
**Ghost:** Massive banks of piled earth and deep ditches form our defenses here at the edge of Calleva. We dug them with simple tools and wooden shovels to mark our tribal territory and keep out thieves. They keep our valuable cattle from wandering off into the deep, wild forests.
**You:** What do you do with your cows?
**Ghost:** Cattle are a man's true wealth here, giving us milk, meat, and strength to plough the soil. My beasts are small and hardy, with dark coats and curved horns, well-suited to our woodland pastures. We keep them safe within the earthworks when the winter wolves grow hungry.
**You:** How do you use the leather and horn?
**Ghost:** Thick cattle hide is scraped clean to make tough leather for horse tack and cloaks. We hollow out the black horns to fashion drinking horns and grease containers. Every part of the animal serves a useful purpose in our home.
**You:** Do beasts sleep inside the earthworks?
**Ghost:** Timber enclosures thatched with gorse keep the freezing rain off my black cattle. We lay fresh straw down daily so they do not stand in cold mud. Their body heat keeps the pen warm through the darkest winter nights.
**You:** Do you have crops near your roundhouse?
**Ghost:** Farewell then. Mind the mud by the dykes as you go.

### Walk 2

**Ghost:** Welcome back! The cattle are fed and the hearth is burning bright. It is good to see a familiar face again. Come, rest your feet by the fire.
**You:** What kind of food do people eat here?
**Ghost:** Grains from our fields nourish us best. We grind barley into coarse flour for pottage or bake flat bread on hearthstones. Fresh milk, garden herbs, and occasional salted pork fill our wooden bowls, giving us energy for hard work in the fields.
**You:** Do you have crops near your roundhouse?
**Ghost:** Barley and spelt wheat are the main crops we plant in our cleared patches of land. We must weed them by hand to ensure a good harvest for the winter months. If the gods are good, we have enough grain to fill our clay storage jars and even trade a little.
**You:** Do you use money?
**Ghost:** Coins of gold, silver, and iron are used when we deal with wealthy merchants or neighboring chieftains. They are stamped with symbols of our land, like fertile wheat ears or swift galloping horses. Most of our local trade is still done by swapping goods directly, friend to friend.
**You:** How does iron help on your farm?
**Ghost:** Heavy farm work needs strong metal, so we rely on iron implements. Sharp axes let us fell trees for building walls, while iron shears keep our sheep fleeces neat. Every blade is hammered out by skilled craftsmen who live near the centre of our settlement.
**You:** Where do your clay jars come from?
**Ghost:** Our daily vessels are fashioned from river clay and dried in the summer sun before firing. We use these sturdy pots to hold water, store our harvested barley, and keep our sheep's milk cool. It is a craft we teach our children so they can help the household.
**You:** What do you get from Gaul?
**Ghost:** May the path be easy beneath your feet. Safe travels, friend.

### Walk 3

**Ghost:** You have returned to Calleva then. The weather has been kind to our crops today. What else would you like to know about our quiet life here?

**Ghost:** Segovax is my name, a plain cattle farmer of the Atrebates tribe. I tend my beasts near the great earthwork dykes of Calleva. My days are spent driving cows to pasture, mending timber fencing, and watching over the herd. It is hard work, but good honest living.
**You:** What is your home like?
**Ghost:** My home is a sturdy roundhouse sitting near the outer dykes. It features thick wattle-and-daub walls, a tall thatched conical roof, and a stone hearth right in the middle. Warm smoke rises inside, keeping the insects away and keeping us dry through winter.
**You:** Can you tell me about your home life with family?
**Ghost:** My family operates as one unit from dawn till dark. Younger kin drive sheep to high pastures, while elders grind grain or mend woven garments. We share stories around the hearth at dusk, resting our tired limbs after busy hours of labor.
**You:** How do you celebrate feasts?
**Ghost:** Great feasts are held when the seasons change, bringing all our kin together inside the earthworks. We thank the unseen powers for keeping our cattle safe from the wolves and the winter cold. Everyone brings something to share, from fresh barley bread to roasted woodland game.
**You:** What instruments do people play at festivals?
**Ghost:** Music plays a big part in our seasonal celebrations. Musicians blow whistles carved from elder branches and strum simple stringed instruments made of wood and gut. Everyone claps along, laughing, telling grand tales, and dancing in wide circles until the flames burn down to glowing embers.
**You:** How do the seasons affect Calleva?
**Ghost:** May the path be easy beneath your feet. Safe travels, friend.

**Ghost:** Ah, you are back before the evening mist settles over the dykes. My kin and I were just finishing the chores. Sit and talk a while.
**You:** How do you buy things here?
**Ghost:** We trade using shiny iron bars and small metal coins struck with images of horses and wheat ears. They are made by our metalworkers right here in the region to help us trade. For daily matters, however, we often just barter a sheep or grain for what we need.
**You:** Do you meet foreign merchants?
**Ghost:** Men from Gaul cross the wide water to trade with our folk here in the woods. They seek our strong British hunting dogs and fat cattle, giving us bright bronze trinkets and strong wine. My neighbours always gather to see what strange, fine goods they bring to our market.
**You:** Do you have crops near your roundhouse?
**Ghost:** Our small fields yield precious spelt wheat and barley, which we clear from the thick woods with our iron axes. Each year we dig the soil to plant the seed, hoping the birds do not steal our future bread. It keeps us busy from spring sowing right through to the autumn harvest.
**You:** What are winter and summer like here?
**Ghost:** Changing seasons govern everything an Atrebates farmer does. Spring calls us to plough sticky fields and watch over newborn calves in green pastures. Summer means endless field work, whereas winter brings damp cold, forcing us to gather close around the hearth fire while woodsmoke fills the rafters.
**You:** What is winter like around the dykes?
**Ghost:** Cold mud is our main trouble when winter storms sweep across the woods. Water pools in deep cart ruts, freezing hard on sharp nights. We keep livestock close inside fenced yards, scattering dry straw across the ground so their hooves stay rot-free until spring sunshine dries the earth.
**You:** Tell me about calving season.
**Ghost:** Go safely. You are always welcome by my hearth.

### Walk 20

**Ghost:** Welcome back! The cattle are fed and the hearth is burning bright. It is good to see a familiar face again. Come, rest your feet by the fire.
**You:** Can you tell me about your daily meals?
**Ghost:** We live on thick pottage boiled in earthenware clay pots. Spelt flour makes good flat cakes, which we eat with cheese and stewed greens. When cattle are slaughtered in autumn, we feast on roasted meat, but grain remains our main daily food.
**You:** What are your clothes made of?
**Ghost:** We wear heavy woollen tunics and cloaks, spun from our sheep's fleece and dyed with woodland plants. The cloth is thick enough to keep out the damp British drizzle and biting winds. My wife weaves beautiful checked patterns using wool of different natural colours from our flock.
**You:** How do you dye your clothes?
**Ghost:** Soaking wool in plant dyes colours the fleece before we set it upon the loom. The forest offers many shades, from pale yellows to deep, muddy greens and warm reds. Wearing patterned wool keeps us looking smart when visiting neighbouring kin across the countryside.
**You:** Can you describe your roundhouse to me?
**Ghost:** We dwell in a round house built from sturdy oak posts and woven branches. The clay walls stop winter winds, while a central hearth fills the space with light and heat. It is a comfortable dwelling for my kin, warm even in damp weather.
**You:** What do your kin do every day?
**Ghost:** Kinship keeps our household strong. While I tend the cows, others gather firewood, spin wool, or weed crop patches. Children help fetch water from the stream and mind the sheep. Every person has tasks that help our roundhouse run smoothly.
**You:** Do you have big festivals here?
**Ghost:** May the path be easy beneath your feet. Safe travels, friend.
