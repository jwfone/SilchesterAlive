# junia walks

### Walk 1

**Ghost:** Step inside from the street. My name is Junia, and I live right above this storeroom near the east gate. The carts keep rattling past for the market, laden with heavy timber and goods.
**You:** Where do you live in this town?
**Ghost:** I live just over my storeroom near the east gate of Calleva. From my window, I can watch the travelers heading out on the road to London, which makes me feel connected to the wider world.
**You:** How do you remember what you have sold?
**Ghost:** Every single morning I count our jars and scratch the numbers carefully onto a wax tablet. It is important to know exactly how much oil remains before the market opens. If my figures are wrong, I might run out of stock before the next wagons arrive.
**You:** What is a wax tablet like?
**Ghost:** My tablet is a flat piece of wood with a hollow center filled with dark, melted beeswax. I use a pointed bronze stylus to scratch my marks into the soft surface. When I make a mistake, I simply rub the wax smooth with the flat end of my tool.
**You:** Where is the wooden forum?
**Ghost:** The forum is a grand wooden square where the town merchants set up their stalls. People go there to hear the latest news, settle arguments before the magistrates, and buy fine goods from all over the empire.
**You:** Is the forum made entirely of wood?
**Ghost:** Oak posts hold up the tiled roofs of the shops and offices around the central yard. It is a fine, neat square, though we must be very careful with fires since the timber walls could burn easily.
**You:** What are travellers saying about the Emperor?
**Ghost:** May your feet stay dry and your lamp burn brightly until we meet again.

### Walk 2

**Ghost:** It is good to see you again. I have just finished checking the seals on my large earthenware jars. Did you need a fresh measure of oil, or more news from the road?
**You:** What is your name and what do you sell?
**Ghost:** My name is Junia and I am an oil merchant. Having traveled from the warm south, I now sell precious olive oil to the townspeople. I measure it out carefully from great clay jars for cooking, washing, and filling small clay lamps.
**You:** Where did you grow up?
**Ghost:** Baetica is my homeland, a place of bright blue skies and shimmering heat. The hillsides there are covered in silver-leaved olive groves, quite unlike these thick, dark British woods that seem to hold the damp forever.
**You:** Tell me about the river barges in Baetica.
**Ghost:** Broad barges carried thousands of bulbous amphorae down the shining Guadalquivir river. Oarsmen rowed steadily past sun-drenched estates, guiding loads down toward the coastal seaports where huge sailing ships waited. Waterways carried our wealth outwards to nourish towns across the empire.
**You:** How is the oil stored here?
**Ghost:** We store the oil in massive, round clay amphorae that are very heavy to lift. Each container is sealed with a tough wax plug to keep the contents fresh. They are stacked neatly in rows downstairs, waiting for the local townspeople to come and buy their daily portions.
**You:** How heavy are these clay jars?
**Ghost:** Lifting a full amphora takes the strength of two strong men, as they weigh more than a grown sheep. I cannot move them alone without risking a terrible spill. Instead, I slide them slowly across the dirt floor using thick wooden rollers and hemp ropes.
**You:** What do you use to measure the oil?
**Ghost:** A safe journey through the town. Return whenever you have need of sweet oil.

### Walk 3

**Ghost:** Back so soon from the muddy streets? Shake the British damp from your cloak. The smell of rich Baetican oil makes this room feel almost as warm as my homeland.
**You:** Is the market very busy?
**Ghost:** The town comes alive when country folk arrive to buy oil and sell their sheep and grain. Our street grid fills with noisy crowds, and my little shop by the London road gets very busy with shoppers clutching their copper coins.
**You:** How do people pay for your olive oil?
**Ghost:** Trading here requires proper Roman money, which we count out on my wooden counter. Little bronze coins are best for small kitchen portions, while silver buys the finest grade. I always ring them on stone to hear if they are genuine.
**You:** Where do you keep your money safe at night?
**Ghost:** Hidden under timber floorboards near my bed lies a stout box carved of seasoned oak and pinned with bronze studs. It is far too heavy for any nimble cutpurse to lift quietly down the ladder. Each evening I count my day's earnings before sliding the iron bolt securely home.
**You:** Can people trick you with fake money in your shop?
**Ghost:** Bouncing a silver denarius against my smooth stone slab makes a clear, high ringing chime if the metal is pure. Bad coins made of tin or lead plated with wash make only a dull clatter. I also weigh them upon balanced pans to confirm the coin is properly full-weight.
**You:** What do you use to measure the oil?
**Ghost:** Bright metal measures help me share the liquid correctly. I own several sizes, from a small tot up to a full sextarius. Even when the morning shop is bustling with folk from the market, I never rush the measuring, so each buyer receives their rightful portion.
**You:** What sits on your workbench beside the oil?
**Ghost:** Farewell for now. Mind the deep wagon ruts along the London road.

Men with iron spades shovel loose flint into wagon ruts and ram it firm with heavy wooden mallets. A well-drained road is the lifeline of our town, so everyone respects the maintenance crews.
**You:** What animals pull your oil deliveries?
**Ghost:** Teams of patient mules drag the heavy carts all the way from the nearest quay. Drivers shout and crack leather reins while wheels grind against loose flint stones. **Ghost:** You return just as the market bells quieten. I am resting my tally slate for a moment. Tell me, how fares your day in our busy town?
**You:** Can you tell me about the London road?
**Ghost:** My store sits right beside the busy gravel highway that leads straight to the port of London. Just outside the east gate, travelers pass by constantly on foot, on horseback, or steering loaded carts. It is a noisy spot, but wonderful for selling oil to folk entering our growing market town.
**You:** What animals pull your oil deliveries?
**Ghost:** Wooden carts laden high with imported jars arrive after days on the rough roads. Reliable pack mules strain into their leather collars to pull every load. It takes great strength to heave such cargo over hilltops, but carters know how to manage the beasts and keep the valuable oil safe.
**You:** Is it hard to bring oil from Hispania?
**Ghost:** From the sunny groves of Baetica, the oil is sealed in heavy clay pots. These pots travel by water and road for many weeks, handled by many strong sailors and carters before I unpack them.
**You:** Do the jars break when ships roll in big waves?
**Ghost:** Deep below the deck, hundreds of round pots rest upright together in beds of river gravel and chopped reeds. This snug packing prevents any jolting during tempestuous swells. When sea water splashes overhead, the thick clay protects our rich cargo until the crew reaches the calm river mouth safely.
**You:** What happens when the ships reach the London docks?
**Ghost:** Wharf workers rig sturdy timber cranes with thick hemp ropes along the London riverside to hoist each amphora from the damp hold. It takes two burly dockers just to guide one swinging jar safely onto the gravel quayside, ready for the carters to inspect and haul westward toward Calleva.
**You:** How do drivers look after their mules?
**Ghost:** May your feet stay dry and your lamp burn brightly until we meet again.

### Walk 20

**Ghost:** It is good to see you again. I have just finished checking the seals on my large earthenware jars. Did you need a fresh measure of oil, or more news from the road?
**You:** Tell me about your oil containers.
**Ghost:** Round clay jars, almost as tall as myself, line the walls of my store. These strong vessels travel across the seas to bring the taste of my homeland to Calleva. Lifting them is hard, back-breaking work, so I use sturdy ropes and rollers to move them about.
**You:** Do you count your jars every day?
**Ghost:** Counting the stock is my very first chore when the sun rises over the London road. I mark the Roman numerals into soft beeswax to keep a tidy ledger of my trade. It is the only way a sensible merchant can run a proper shop in Calleva.
**You:** How do people pay for your olive oil?
**Ghost:** Buyers pay for their oil using bright bronze asses or shiny silver denarii. I prefer the small, heavy coins with the Emperor's face stamped clearly on them. Every coin is counted carefully and kept in my strongbox under the floorboards upstairs.
**You:** Where do you keep your money safe at night?
**Ghost:** My savings rest inside an iron-plated casket tucked away upstairs beneath the bedroom floor. Only my heavy bedframe sitting atop the timber hatch reveals nothing to wandering eyes. Sleeping directly over my earnings brings peaceful nights, knowing the bronze and silver remain untouched until the market opens at sunrise.
**You:** Where is your house in Calleva?
**Ghost:** My home is a simple room above the shop floor. It is very handy, as I only have to climb a wooden ladder to go from my daily tallies to my warm bed, though the smell of oil is always with me.
**You:** What can you see outside your window?
**Ghost:** Farewell for now. Mind the deep wagon ruts along the London road.
