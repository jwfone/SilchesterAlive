# elen walks

### Walk 1

**Ghost:** Hullo there. You walk softly, like the sheep on the grass. I live here in Calleva with my family, and I know all the best places where the roof tiles do not leak.
**You:** Who are you and what do you do every day?
**Ghost:** People call me Elen. I live here with my family, spending my days carrying water jugs and watching over the newborn lambs. It is busy work, but I like brushing the soft fleece. When the sun goes down, mother teaches me ancient songs beside our warm hearth.
**You:** Can you describe a normal day here?
**Ghost:** Dawn wakes me up before the sun is high. I run to the well for water, check the small lambs, and stir our breakfast pottage over the hearth. Later, I help Father watch the sheep and mend our simple fences. Dusk brings us all back inside.
**You:** Do you have to carry heavy water buckets?
**Ghost:** Fetching clean water takes strong arms. Every morning I carry heavy wooden buckets from our well, which runs slower than it used to. Mother says we must turn the winch carefully so the rope does not slip. It is a long walk back, but the water tastes sweet and cold.
**You:** Do your arms get tired lifting water?
**Ghost:** Hauling water tests my strength every single dawn. My hands get red and sore from gripping the rough iron handle, so I swap sides often. When the bucket is full, I must tread carefully along the gravel path so I do not spill a single drop before reaching our doorway.
**You:** What food goes into your cooking pot?
**Ghost:** Barley porridge bubbles in our pot almost every single morning. We toss in garden leeks, chopped cabbage, and wild herbs gathered near the great walls. Sometimes we have fresh sheep's milk or bake round flatbreads directly upon clean, heated hearthstones.
**You:** What does your family trade with neighbours?
**Ghost:** Safe travels to you. May the rain stay off until you reach home.

### Walk 2

**Ghost:** You came back to Calleva! That is good. The little speckled lamb is sleeping now, so I have plenty of time to talk or show you the sturdy old walls nearby.
**You:** What is your doll made from?
**Ghost:** Scraps of pale linen make up her little body. I helped stuff her with leftover tufts of sheared lamb fleece until she was plump and soft. She wears a small woollen kirtle that I hemmed myself. She listens to all my secrets in the quiet evenings.
**You:** Do you go to school or have a tutor?
**Ghost:** Learning happens right at our fireside rather than from parchment or a schoolmaster. Mother hums the ancient tunes while she cards the wool, and I echo each note. The words stay in my heart easily, telling of brave shepherds, cold winter frosts, and the bright summer sun over Calleva.
**You:** Which rhymes do you say at home?
**Ghost:** Stirring the pot goes faster when we chant the counting verse. Mother hums about five speckled hens while I turn the heavy wooden spoon, and we have another silly rhyme for sweeping the hearthstones clean. It makes hard daily chores feel like merriment instead of dull labor.
**You:** How do you give thanks each day in Calleva?
**Ghost:** Quiet words of thanks start our day before any work begins. Mother prays softly for our little home, the flock, and bread upon our board. We stand together by the open doorway while the sky turns pink, asking for peaceful days and strength for our daily tasks.
**You:** Do you pray together as a family every morning?
**Ghost:** Early dawn brings our family together around the quiet hearthstone. We fold our hands while mother gives thanks for the rising sun and our sturdy roof. It is a calm, sweet moment that prepares father for the fields and reminds me to be helpful all day long.
**You:** What do you do each day?
**Ghost:** Goodbye for now. Mind your step near the old stones, and come back soon.

### Walk 3

**Ghost:** Hullo again. I was just mending a tiny woollen cloak for my doll by the doorway. Come out of the wind and tell me how your day has been going.
**You:** Do you help your father with the sheep?
**Ghost:** Cold mists often chill the meadow at dawn, so I gather the shivering lambs into my cloak. When they shake, I hold them tight and blow warm breath onto their faces. Afterwards, I pick prickly burrs from their soft wool so their mothers let them nurse safely without hurting.
**You:** How do you turn sheep wool into warm yarn?
**Ghost:** I help card sheep wool and roll it into bundles while Mother turns her clay drop-spindle. We wash the greasy fleece in clear well water first, picking out thistle prickles. Then Mother spins the soft fluff into neat, strong yarn for our winter garments.
**You:** What does your family trade with neighbours?
**Ghost:** Most folk simply swap useful goods across their low garden fences. If our hens lay well, we give eggs to the smith for fixing our heavy hoe. People rarely bother with old metal coins now, because you cannot eat metal or weave it into a thick winter cloak.
**You:** What tools does your father use for shepherding?
**Ghost:** A strong wooden staff with a curved top is Father's main tool. He uses it to steady himself on steep earthen banks and reach trapped lambs. In his belt pouch, he carries curved iron blades for shearing and a little bone whistle to direct our dog.
**You:** How do the iron shears cut the wool?
**Ghost:** Careful hands are needed when working the springy iron shears. Father sits a sheep on its rump and cuts the fleece in one big blanket of wool. He sharpens the curved blades every evening, keeping them bright with a drop of rendered tallow against the damp.
**You:** How do you clean thorns from sheep wool?
**Ghost:** Goodbye for now. Mind your step near the old stones, and come back soon.

### Walk 4

**Ghost:** Hullo again. I was just mending a tiny woollen cloak for my doll by the doorway. Come out of the wind and tell me how your day has been going.
**You:** Do you help your father with the sheep?
**Ghost:** Out in our grass pens, weak lambs need extra looking after. I sit on the dry turf, tucking them near my knees to keep the bitter wind off. Once their little ears warm up, my fingers comb away thorns and dirt so their curly wool stays clean and soft.
**You:** What tools does your father use for shepherding?
**Ghost:** Father carries a tall hazel crook that he smoothed with sand. It helps him catch straying ewes by the leg without hurting them. He also keeps heavy iron shears in a leather wrap, ready to snip away thick dirty fleece when the warm weather comes around.
**You:** What does your father do with his crook?
**Ghost:** Bending the wood over steam made the crook's curved handle. It is tall enough for Father to lean on while counting sheep in the pasture. The wide hook is smooth and rounded, designed to guide stubborn ewes without bruising their fleece or scraping their soft skin.
**You:** Do you use money or trade goods in town?
**Ghost:** Swapping goods is how everyone gets what they need in Calleva. We bring washed wool and dried herbs to trade for pottery bowls or sharp needles. A neighbor might give us sweet honey if we help shear their sheep, which is far better than keeping dusty old coins in a box.
**You:** Are there many people living in the town now?
**Ghost:** Grassy patches grow between some of the stone road gutters today. The town is far quieter than it used to be, yet plenty of families remain inside the walls. We greet the smith, the tanner, and other shepherds as we bring the sheep along the lanes.
**You:** Are there houses here where nobody lives anymore?
**Ghost:** Safe travels to you. May the rain stay off until you reach home.

**Ghost:** I am Elen, and I have eight years now. Mostly I fetch heavy buckets from the well, or run after father's sheep when they wander off through the weeds. My arms grow strong carrying water, but if I finish early, I sit by the doorway with my little rag doll.
**You:** Tell me about your family at home.
**Ghost:** There are four of us under our roof. I live with my mother, my shepherd father, and my grandmother, who tells me how the town used to be. We share our hearth fire, mend our warm clothes together, and keep each other safe inside the sturdy town walls.
**You:** Who teaches you your verses and songs?
**Ghost:** Learning happens right at our fireside rather than from parchment or a schoolmaster. Mother hums the ancient tunes while she cards the wool, and I echo each note. The words stay in my heart easily, telling of brave shepherds, cold winter frosts, and the bright summer sun over Calleva.
**You:** Do you say prayers with your family at home?
**Ghost:** Quiet words of thanks start our day before any work begins. Mother prays softly for our little home, the flock, and bread upon our board. We stand together by the open doorway while the sky turns pink, asking for peaceful days and strength for our daily tasks.
**You:** Can you describe a normal day here?
**Ghost:** Sunlight peeking through the shutter marks the start of my work. First comes bringing water from the well, then scattering grain for hens and tending the sheep. By midday, I practice my songs or play with Morwen before helping prepare our evening meal.
**You:** How do you help your parents early on?
**Ghost:** Farewell. I must go check on the lambs before dusk settles over Calleva.

### Walk 19

**Ghost:** It is nice to see you walking our streets once more. I have finished carrying the water bucket from the well, so we can sit together on the stone step.
**You:** What does your house look like inside?
**Ghost:** We dwell in a sturdy stone house built long ago, sheltered inside the great town walls. Rain sometimes creeps through where old tiles cracked, but father patched the roof with thick thatch and turf. Inside, our hearth fire keeps the chill away from our sleeping blankets on cold nights.
**You:** Can you tell me about your rag doll?
**Ghost:** Stitched from leftover woven cloth, my little doll goes with me everywhere. Mother shaped her arms and legs, and I filled her belly with scraps of sheared fleece. Her face is sewn with grey wool thread. She keeps me company when I watch the pastures.
**You:** Who teaches you your verses and songs?
**Ghost:** Books are things I have never seen, yet my head is full of tunes. Sitting by the glowing embers, Mother sings old rhymes until I can chant them back. I sing them to my doll Morwen while folding linen, or softly out in the fields when watching the sheep.
**You:** How do you give thanks each day in Calleva?
**Ghost:** Quiet words of thanks start our day before any work begins. Mother prays softly for our little home, the flock, and bread upon our board. We stand together by the open doorway while the sky turns pink, asking for peaceful days and strength for our daily tasks.
**You:** Who lives with you in your house?
**Ghost:** Our family works together every single day. Father tends the sheep outside the gates while mother bakes bread and keeps the house tidy. Grandmother sits by the hearth spinning fine yarn, telling me wonderful old tales while I brush the fleece and play quietly with my rag doll.
**You:** What does your father do with the sheep?
**Ghost:** Safe travels to you. May the rain stay off until you reach home.

### Walk 20

**Ghost:** You came back to Calleva! That is good. The little speckled lamb is sleeping now, so I have plenty of time to talk or show you the sturdy old walls nearby.
**You:** What do you do when a lamb is cold?
**Ghost:** Early morning frost can trouble newborn sheep, which is why I help Father right after sunrise. I cradle the smallest ones against my chest until their strength returns. Checking every scrap of fleece for sharp brambles takes patience, but it keeps the little flock contented and thriving.
**You:** Do you take the flock out to the grass?
**Ghost:** We let the flock pasture along the grassy banks right beside the high walls. The sheep love the tender weeds that sprout between old stones. When the sun is high, Father leads them through the open gateway into the wide, sunny fields outside our quiet town.
**You:** Is it hard to watch all the sheep together?
**Ghost:** Watching the whole flock takes sharp eyes, especially out in the open meadow. We keep the ewes near the old stonework so they cannot easily scatter. If one wanders, Father uses his long crook to gently guide it right back to the green path.
**You:** Who built the great stone ring?
**Ghost:** Tall grey flint rises high around our streets, stacked up in what folk call the old work. Nobody living today laid those stones; they were set in firm mortar long before Grandmother was small. They keep the night winds and roving wolves outside our quiet, safe dwellings.
**You:** Are there many people living in the town now?
**Ghost:** Fewer children run through the square than in my grandmother's youth. Even so, our town is not deserted; many stone homes have cheerful fires burning inside. You can always find someone mending fences, baking bread, or bartering wool on a bright morning.
**You:** What happens to the buildings that people left behind?
**Ghost:** Goodbye for now. Mind your step near the old stones, and come back soon.
