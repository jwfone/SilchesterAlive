# bassa walks

### Walk 1

**Ghost:** Halt a moment, let the dogs catch your scent so they know you as a friend. I am Bassa the huntsman. We keep our watch out here among the oak and bracken, well clear of the silent grey ruins.
**You:** Who are you and what do you do here?
**Ghost:** Men know me as Bassa the huntsman. Day in and day out, I care for my master's dogs along the fringes of the old stone walls. It is healthy, outdoor work, full of cold morning air, running across wet grass, and watching the weather turn.
**You:** Tell me about the noble who owns the dogs.
**Ghost:** His great timber hall sits a long journey from here, well away from these quiet ruins. He owns these lands and the pack, trusting me to keep his hunting beasts strong and well trained. I see his riders only when they come bearing flour or collecting salted meat.
**You:** Tell me about your clothing.
**Ghost:** Tough wool and leather are my best armour against thorns. Thick woollen hose, a sturdy linen shirt, and a greased leather coat keep brambles from tearing my skin while chasing after hounds. On my feet, stout turnshoes lined with dry moss keep the forest damp away.
**You:** Do you hunt the same in summer and winter?
**Ghost:** Each part of the year brings a different task. When springtime comes, fresh litters tumble about the kennel, whilst summer heat dries the trails and slows our pace. Bitter winter nights mean heaping split logs high to keep the cold wind from biting the hounds.
**You:** What kind of meals do you have each day?
**Ghost:** Rye flour and water make cakes baked on the glowing embers. With those, I boil leeks, wild garlic, and dried yellow peas into a thick mash. Salted fat from the farmsteads adds grease to the water, giving me plenty of strength for working hounds all day.
**You:** How do you stay warm at night?
**Ghost:** Walk softly through the heather, friend. Swift and Ash send you safely on your way.

### Walk 2

**Ghost:** Back again, are you? Swift caught your scent on the wind before you even cleared the hazel thicket. Come rest your legs near the trees, for the trail has been peaceful this morning.
**You:** Tell me about your home in the woods.
**Ghost:** Out in the forest border is where I make my dwelling. Stout timber posts and turf keep the frost out when autumn bites. The old walls stand nearby, but I prefer the rustle of leaves overhead to sleeping among fallen masonry with no true roof to shield me.
**You:** How do you stay warm at night?
**Ghost:** Bright flames bring life to the dim hut. As shadows gather outside the door, I toss on dried oak limbs to chase the dampness away. The hounds settle right at my feet, sharing their own animal heat while the crackling wood warms our weary bones.
**You:** How do you gather logs for the cold nights?
**Ghost:** Good seasoned wood is worth the trouble of gathering. Green wood only hisses and fills the room with thick grey smoke that makes our eyes water. I look for dead limbs snapped by autumn gales, trimming them to fit neatly into my shallow hearth.
**You:** Are the hounds peaceful when sleeping together?
**Ghost:** They crowd together without any snapping once bellies are full. Old hounds take the warmest spots nearest the embers, while younger ones curl around my feet like living blankets. Their soft twitches show they are coursing hares through their dreams as they sleep.
**You:** Do the hunting hounds get meat to eat?
**Ghost:** A heavy iron pot simmers over the hearth all afternoon for them. I mix ground grain with kitchen scraps, trimmings of deer fat, and boiled marrow bones pounded flat. Good dogs need hearty fare to run hard. When the steam rises, their tails thump the earth in eager chorus.
**You:** Why do you crush the bones first?
**Ghost:** Walk softly through the heather, friend. Swift and Ash send you safely on your way.

### Walk 3

**Ghost:** Well met once more beneath the green boughs. Ash has just finished his dish of broth, and I was about to comb the burs from his coat. Have you been walking far along the ditch today?
**You:** What are your hunting dogs like?
**Ghost:** Every hound has its own name and true nature. There is swift Swift-foot, sturdy Bran, and keen-nosed Bramble, who never loses a path. I spend hours combing their coats and training their ears so they heed my whistle even when running fast through heavy brush.
**You:** How do you catch hares with your dogs?
**Ghost:** Hares are nimble runners, so only my fastest hounds can match their turn. We creep quietly along the edges of the brambles until one springs from hiding. Then I uncouple the brace, shouting them on as they dash across the clearings, bounding over tussocks until the run is done.
**You:** How does the dog catch up with the running hare?
**Ghost:** A proper run lasts but a few breathless moments across the scrub. The hare doubles back on its own tracks to fool the dogs, darting toward the tumbled flint ruins. If it reaches the heavy bramble patches in time, it safely slips away beneath the thorns and wins its freedom.
**You:** Do the dogs get a reward after catching one?
**Ghost:** Walking over to the dogs, I check their pads for thorns and sharp flints. A clean catch means fresh stew for the pot tonight. The hounds get their reward of giblets while the meat is still warm, then we walk gently back under the trees to rest their tired legs.
**You:** Can your hounds smell things far away?
**Ghost:** Wet ferns and cool air keep a trail fresh for hours. Gentle breezes bring me word of wandering beasts before my eyes ever catch sight of them. The hounds will freeze, quivering as sweet scents brush their whiskers, showing me which path we ought to follow through the dense briars.
**You:** Does rain wash away animal tracks?
**Ghost:** Walk softly through the heather, friend. Swift and Ash send you safely on your way.

### Walk 4

**Ghost:** Well met once more beneath the green boughs. Ash has just finished his dish of broth, and I was about to comb the burs from his coat. Have you been walking far along the ditch today?
**You:** How do you spend your mornings and afternoons?
**Ghost:** Rising at first light, I greet each hound and inspect their paws. Next comes scouting the forest edges for fresh droppings or broken twigs. Midday brings practice with the leads and tracking, keeping the young beasts sharp until dusk calls us back to the warm fire.
**You:** What tools do you carry when you go hunting?
**Ghost:** Tough rawhide leashes stay coiled over my arm so I can hold the hounds close when needed. My stave vaults me over muddy ditches, while my horn summons every dog back to heel. These plain, sturdy things are all a huntsman truly needs out under the broad woodland boughs.
**You:** How do your dogs hear your horn so far away?
**Ghost:** Smooth horn gives me three distinct calls that my dogs have known since they were little pups. It never clogs with mud or rusts in the rain, making it the most reliable tool I carry. Even across the great stone walls, that merry tune brings them bounding toward me.
**You:** How do you keep warm and dry out here?
**Ghost:** Heavy home-spun wool dyed with bark brown shields me from wind. Over that, I wear a sleeveless hide coat rubbed with sheep tallow to repel the wet. Nothing fancy hangs in my hut, only durable garments that can survive mud, claw marks, and wet forest weather.
**You:** Why do you put grease on your leather coat?
**Ghost:** Smearing boiled fat across thick deerskin stops water soaking through to my linen tunic. Thorn bushes tear ordinary wool to ribbons, but greased leather turns their barbs away. It bears many claw marks from playful whelps, yet it remains the finest shield I own against wind, rain, and briars.
**You:** Why do you put moss inside your footwear?
**Ghost:** May the wind stay at your back and the woodland paths keep firm underfoot.

### Walk 5

**You:** Where do you live out here?
**Ghost:** Under the thick green boughs stands my little hut of wattle and thatch. Folk say the empty streets inside the walls are drafty places, so I stay well outside. Here beneath the oaks, my hounds and I rest warm enough through the night upon dry rushes and ferns.
**You:** What do you do every day?
**Ghost:** Daylight begins with the dogs barking for their breakfast. Once they are fed and watered, I step outside to test the breeze and look for prints along the woodland verge. If the weather holds fair, we range out towards the fields, ever watchful for any deer or hare.
**You:** How do you finish your workday?
**Ghost:** Sunset finds me latching the kennel door tight against wandering foxes. Once the hounds are curled together asleep, I boil my own supper pot over the hearth. It is quiet out here at night, save for the breeze whispering high in the tall oak branches.
**You:** What are your earliest tasks when waking up?
**Ghost:** Before anything else, I scoop clean water into the dog troughs and ladle out boiled meal. The hounds whine with eagerness until every dish is set down. After they eat, I check their ears for thorns and glance at the sky to see which way the clouds scud.

Only nettles, briars, and shy woodland creatures walk the pathways where ancient builders once laid their heavy flint courses.

**You:** Do your hounds ever help find lost sheep?
**Ghost:** Neighbours sometimes come calling when a ewe strays into the tangle of briars. A wandering sheep leaves little traces on the gorse, and my broad-nosed dogs can smell her wool from far away. They lead me patiently through the undergrowth until we discover the frightened beast safe and sound.
**You:** Do other people live near your hut?
**Ghost:** Woodcutters and charcoal burners keep their own low dwellings down the winding track. While the ancient town inside the stones sits empty, honest folk work the fringe. We share a horn of ale, swap warnings of muddy bogs, and watch out for one another's hearths through the year.
**You:** Do you use coins to buy things here?
**Ghost:** May the wind stay at your back and the woodland paths keep firm underfoot.
