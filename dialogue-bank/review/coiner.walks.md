# coiner walks

### Walk 1

**Ghost:** Step carefully past the hot embers. I cut the bronze and iron tools that stamp wealth for the Atrebates. Come, watch me turn melted gold into beautiful coin.
**You:** Who are you and what is your job here?
**Ghost:** My name is Addedomaros, a maker of coin dies for the king's wealth. I carve hard iron punches to create the patterns stamped onto pure gold. It is skilled work, keeping the royal mark clear so everyone knows the coins come from our famous settlement.
**You:** Why are cattle and gold so important to kings?
**Ghost:** Great kings measure their standing by vast cattle herds and bright gold coins. Owning farmland, many cows, and heavy gold lets a ruler reward loyal warriors. When I strike fresh gold coins, I help our king display his grandeur to all tribes.
**You:** What do you exchange with merchants from Gaul?
**Ghost:** Sailing ships cross the grey salt water from Gaul to reach our shores. Foreign merchants bring tall clay jars of red wine and fine glass beads. In return, our king trades shiny gold coins, grain, stout iron tools, and sturdy hunting hounds.
**You:** What garments keep you warm in Calleva?
**Ghost:** Spun wool forms our primary garments here in Calleva. We wear long tunics dyed with plants, wrapped in thick cloaks against the damp air. A sturdy bronze brooch pinned at the shoulder keeps my heavy mantle pinned tight while I swing my iron coin hammer.
**You:** Are your tools made of metal?
**Ghost:** Metal is my life, from the bronze hammer I swing to the iron dies that stamp the gold. I use fine abrasive sand and hard stones to polish the metal faces smooth. Each tool is kept clean and dry in my wooden chest, safe from the damp winter air.
**You:** What is the step by step way to make coins?
**Ghost:** Farewell, friend. I must return to my iron dies and gold.

### Walk 2

**Ghost:** You return to Calleva, my friend. The forge fire is still warm, and I am carving a proud horse onto a new iron die. Come, tell me what news you bring.
**You:** What is the step by step way to make coins?
**Ghost:** To fashion a coin, I soften gold over the hearth until it forms neat little beads. I place one bead onto a carved iron die set in a wooden block, put the top die over it, and strike once with my bronze hammer. The gold takes the stamp instantly.
**You:** Where do you do your work?
**Ghost:** Step inside my round hut and you will smell charcoal smoke and warm metal. The walls are wattle plastered with clay, topped by a tall reed thatch roof. There is just enough light from the open doorway to see my tiny iron dies as I work.
**You:** Is the hearth fire dangerous inside a thatched hut?
**Ghost:** A hot central fire is the heart of my work. Using reed blowpipes, I pump air into red-hot charcoal coals until they roar. The stone hearth ring keeps the fire contained safely away from my wooden workbench and straw bedding.
**You:** How do the seasons change your work here?
**Ghost:** Frost turns the grass white outside our thatched roofs each winter morning. Cold draughts make my hands stiff, making tiny carving work difficult. I bundle up in thick woollen cloaks and burn extra fuel so the gold flows smoothly into clay cups despite the bitter chill.
**You:** How do you thank the gods in your town?
**Ghost:** Folk leave grain offerings in deep clay-lined pits to pray for good harvests and strong herds. We gather when the seasons change, lighting great bonfires and sharing feasts of roasted meat to thank the spirits of the land for keeping our families safe and our crops growing.
**You:** What is your favorite food?
**Ghost:** May your path be safe beyond the town dykes. Mind the cold wind.

### Walk 3

**Ghost:** Good to see you back in my workshop. The king still demands fine coin, so my hammer never stays silent for long. What would you like to ask today?
**You:** Tell me about this place, Calleva.
**Ghost:** Deep earthen ditches and high timber fences protect our settlement here at Calleva. Within these great rings of earth, we build our homes close together. It is a bustling stronghold, safe from forest wolves and raiders who might want to steal our king's fine cattle.
**You:** Who comes from across the sea to trade?
**Ghost:** Sailing ships cross the grey salt water from Gaul to reach our shores. Foreign merchants bring tall clay jars of red wine and fine glass beads. In return, our king trades shiny gold coins, grain, stout iron tools, and sturdy hunting hounds.
**You:** How do you fasten your cloaks?
**Ghost:** Warm tunic shirts and long breeches are what most craftsmen wear daily. Over these, we drape heavy woollen cloaks secured with cast bronze brooches. Such stout clothing protects us well when working beside sparks or walking out in winter rains.
**You:** Do you have family living with you in Silchester?
**Ghost:** My family shares this timber home with me. When I am not carving iron dies, my kinfolk help grind grain for our bread and gather charcoal. We rely on each other for warm clothes, hearty meals, and keeping our modest workshop safe from hungry thieves.
**You:** What do you eat?
**Ghost:** Folk here feast on rich stews of mutton, beans, and wild garlic cooked in bronze cauldrons. We also bake simple barley cakes directly on the hot hearth stones of our roundhouses. Honey is our greatest treat, collected carefully from hollow trees.
**You:** Which beasts are most important to your people?
**Ghost:** Farewell, friend. I must return to my iron dies and gold.

**You:** Where do you do your work?
**Ghost:** Go softly through Calleva. May good fortune follow your steps.

### Walk 19

**Ghost:** Ah, a familiar face amongst the roundhouses. I have just finished melting a fresh clay cup of gold from across the sea. Sit by the fire and speak.
**You:** What is the step by step way to make coins?
**Ghost:** First, I melt gold dust in a tiny clay cup over hot coals. Once it cools into a round pellet, I set it between two carved iron dies. A single heavy blow from my bronze hammer flattens the pellet and presses our detailed marks right into the metal.
**You:** What design is stamped on the king's treasure?
**Ghost:** Our coins carry two great marks: a noble horse stamped on top and golden ears of wheat underneath. These images speak of strength in battle and plenty in the fields, making the money prized by both warrior lords and visiting merchants from afar.
**You:** Are there horses and cows around your workshop?
**Ghost:** Cattle and horses are everywhere in Calleva. Shaggy cows walk the paths between our roundhouses, their bells clanking softly in the breeze. Our noble warriors ride sleek, powerful horses that are bred for speed, and we treat these beasts with great respect because they sustain our daily life.
**You:** How do you thank the gods in your town?
**Ghost:** Festivals are held at harvest time when the barley is gathered in. We bake large loaves of bread and share dark beer around the central fires. Everyone sings to thank the earth spirits, hoping the coming cold winter will not bring sickness to our roundhouses.
**You:** What is winter like inside Calleva?
**Ghost:** Rain and dark skies mark the turning of the year in our settlement. Summer lets me carve outside in the sunlight, but icy months force me close to the hearth. Working gold needs steady heat, so winter days are spent stoking oak embers inside my timber hut.
**You:** Where do you sit when it rains outside?
**Ghost:** Farewell, friend. I must return to my iron dies and gold.

### Walk 20

**Ghost:** You return to Calleva, my friend. The forge fire is still warm, and I am carving a proud horse onto a new iron die. Come, tell me what news you bring.
**You:** Can you show me how gold becomes money?
**Ghost:** Making coins starts with heating gold dust until it runs liquid in a clay cup. I pour small rounded blanks and let them cool. Then I trap a blank between my lower and upper iron dies, striking hard with a heavy mallet to imprint the design.
**You:** What kind of tools do you use for your work?
**Ghost:** I carve iron punches with hard, pointed stones to shape the die faces for stamping. My hand grips a heavy bronze hammer, cast in a clay mould, which I swing down onto the cold iron dies to strike the gold. Without these metal tools, my craft would be impossible.
**You:** Where do you do your work?
**Ghost:** My workshop is a sturdy roundhouse built of timber posts, woven hazel, and thick thatch. Inside, a charcoal hearth glows hot in the center, surrounded by my anvils, iron punches, clay crucibles, and bronze hammer. It keeps the wind out while I craft gold.
**You:** What is your day like?
**Ghost:** My day begins when the forest birds start to sing and the sky turns grey. I quickly stoke the dying embers of my hearth fire and eat a bowl of hot porridge. Then, I spend long hours carving details into my iron coin dies.
**You:** What is your favorite food?
**Ghost:** We eat thick barley porridge sweetened with wild honey from the forest, which fills our bellies well. For feast days, we roast salted beef over the open flames and bake flatbread on flat stones. We wash it all down with herbal beer or water.
**You:** Who comes from across the sea to trade?
**Ghost:** May your path be safe beyond the town dykes. Mind the cold wind.
