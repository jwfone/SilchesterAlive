# enica walks

### Walk 1

**Ghost:** Step into the yard out of the breeze. My name is Enica; I spin and weave here for our master's family. You are very welcome to pause while I wind my spindle and prepare more wool.
**You:** What is this big house like?
**Ghost:** Fine stone walls shelter our courtyard from the dusty street outside. Within, there are cool corridors in summer and hearth fires in winter, surrounded by storehouses, pantries, and sleeping chambers. I know every paved walkway, having swept the corridors and washed the flags since I was a young girl.
**You:** Tell me about the garments worn here.
**Ghost:** Tunics of rough wool are worn by almost everyone here, belted loosely at the waist. When cold winds sweep through the town, we wrap thick rectangular cloaks round ourselves, pinned firmly at the shoulder with a small bronze brooch. They keep out the damp air nicely.
**You:** What happens to your yard work when seasons turn cold?
**Ghost:** When winter damp sets in, my fingers stiffen around the distaff, so we move our stools closer to the cookhouse wall. Summer lets us spread wet wool across gravel to dry under the sun. Rain drives our weaving indoors, where smoky oil lamps cast dim light on the warp threads.
**You:** Tell me about your little room.
**Ghost:** Just beside the busy courtyard rests my humble chamber. There is barely room for more than my straw pallet and a wooden peg for my cloak, but it keeps dry in winter rains. The faint scent of spun yarn always lingers near me while I drift off to sleep.
**You:** What is a regular day like for you?
**Ghost:** First light wakes me before the masters stir, giving me time to untangle the wool. Standing on flagstones all day keeps my shoulders sore, but watching the grey tunic take shape brings quiet satisfaction until darkness bids us blow out the lamps.
**You:** What happens when the workday ends?
**Ghost:** Farewell for now. I shall think of our talk while I finish spinning this fleece into yarn.

### Walk 2

**Ghost:** You have found your way back to my corner of the yard. The shuttle has barely paused, but there is always time to set it down and hear what you have seen in Calleva today.
**You:** Can you tell me about yourself and your work?
**Ghost:** I am Enica, the weaver for this courtyard house. From morning bell until dusk, my fingers work the fleece, smoothing, twisting, and beating the cloth tight. Everything from kitchen cloths to winter cloaks passes through my hands, keeping all under our master's roof warm against the damp autumn chill.
**You:** Do you ever hope to be free?
**Ghost:** Every weaver cherishes dreams between throws of the shuttle. Mine is simple: to be granted my freedom eventually and set up my own timber frame. Folk say patience brings reward, so I serve this family well, trusting that one day I might sell woollen tunics under my own name.
**You:** Where would you put your future loom?
**Ghost:** Bright morning light would spill across its warp threads. My future loom would be narrow enough for one woman to work with ease, pegged together with sturdy ash pins. There I could craft whatever patterns pleased me, perhaps dyeing borders with meadow herbs to delight passing market shoppers.
**You:** How does your loom work in the yard?
**Ghost:** Hanging weights of baked clay do the hardest work, pulling down the long strands so they do not twist. My hands guide the shuttle through the shed, and my bone pin presses the weft close. Without well-balanced weights, the fabric would turn out crooked and loose under the master's eye.
**You:** Where do you get the clay weights for the loom?
**Ghost:** Kitchen hands help me mould them from damp riverbed clay whenever the old ones crack. We shape them into thick rounds with a thumbhole in the middle, then bake them by the hearth until they turn pale pink. Each must weigh the same so the hanging warp stays nicely balanced.
**You:** What do you do with dirty fleeces?
**Ghost:** Farewell for now. I shall think of our talk while I finish spinning this fleece into yarn.

### Walk 3

**Ghost:** It is good to see your face again. I was just thinking of the broth cooking in the kitchen as I worked this border. Have you been walking through the town since we last spoke?
**You:** Who do you share your meals with?
**Ghost:** When work pauses, I sit with the cooks around the hearth. We scoop up thick spelt porridge flavoured with boiled leeks, wild greens, and broad beans. Sometimes a sliver of salt pork or pickled herring adds savoury grease, giving us plenty of strength to manage the afternoon chores.
**You:** Where do you sleep at night?
**Ghost:** Tucked off the yard sits my plain little space. It smells of dry straw and washed fleece, which brings me comfort after dark. My mattress is just a hemp sack stuffed fresh each harvest, but with a rough wool blanket, it keeps the cold stone floor away quite well.
**You:** Can you tell me about yourself and your work?
**Ghost:** I am Enica, the weaver for this courtyard house. From morning bell until dusk, my fingers work the fleece, smoothing, twisting, and beating the cloth tight. Everything from kitchen cloths to winter cloaks passes through my hands, keeping all under our master's roof warm against the damp autumn chill.
**You:** How does your weaving loom work?
**Ghost:** Two tall wooden uprights rest against the walkway wall, holding hundreds of combed cords. Weights of fired clay keep the warp straight so threads do not tangle while my shuttle flies across. It clatters softly through the yard from early morning, growing into a wide blanket before the month ends.
**You:** What is your wooden spindle used for?
**Ghost:** Smooth wooden sticks with round clay rings do all the twisting when we spin the fleece. I give the spindle a quick roll against my thigh, letting it hang and whirl in the air. The twisting draws wool out fine, giving us sturdy spools of thread ready for the dye pot.
**You:** What do you wish for the future?
**Ghost:** Farewell for now. I shall think of our talk while I finish spinning this fleece into yarn.

### Walk 4

**You:** Why do I hear loud hammering in the town centre?

**You:** How do people buy things in the market?
**Ghost:** Trading takes place with small copper coins or simple exchanges of everyday goods. Farmers bringing carts through the gates will gladly take finished homespun tunics in exchange for sacks of dry barley. We manage without gold, so long as folk need warm wool and honest food.
**You:** Do you ever hope to be free?
**Ghost:** One sweet daydream keeps my heart light while I spin. I wish to gain my freedom and weave cloth for myself. Having a small workshop with my own upright loom would be wonderful. Every neat thread I finish brings that quiet prayer just a tiny bit closer to me.
**You:** Who are you and what do you make here?
**Ghost:** My name is Enica. Each day begins with raw fleece, which I spin into neat thread and weave upon my upright loom out in the sheltered yard. It is steady craft, making warm tunics and blankets for the family here, from the earliest sunrise until the yard lanterns are lit.
**You:** Tell me about your little room.
**Ghost:** Behind the covered walkway, my tiny sleeping chamber stays quiet once dusk falls. A simple wooden bench holds my bedding, filled with clean threshing straw that rustles whenever I turn. It is small, yet having my own dry corner to rest my weary bones feels like a true blessing.
**You:** Do you keep anything special in your room?
**Ghost:** Go safely through the streets. I must get back to my shuttle before the daylight fades from the yard.

### Walk 6

**Ghost:** It is good to see your face again. I was just thinking of the broth cooking in the kitchen as I worked this border. Have you been walking through the town since we last spoke?
**You:** What keeps the town safe?
**Ghost:** High grey battlements encircle all our streets and gardens. The stout stone wall around Calleva was built before my time, standing tall for a whole generation to guard the gates. Walking past its flint bands makes one feel safe, knowing the country woods remain securely outside our sturdy town boundary.
**You:** Why do I hear loud hammering in the town centre?
**Ghost:** That great hall in the centre was once for magistrates, folk say. Today it rings with busy smiths pounding red iron on anvils. Smoke pours from their hearths as they mend ploughshares and beat out hinges. I only pass by on errands, but the heat reaches right out into the roadway.
**You:** Do travellers bring stories to the house?
**Ghost:** Carters arriving through the gates bring tales from distant roads. They speak of sticky mud out in the country, troop movements far away, and fresh coins struck across the water. I listen closely while carding fleece by the yard gate, catching every small piece of chatter from the world beyond our high walls.
**You:** Do you ever get days off from weaving?
**Ghost:** Joyful celebrations pause our daily toil for a few precious hours. The mistress leaves wreaths of fresh ivy by the threshold, and the cook turns out warm buns glazed with honey. Sitting quietly without bleeding fingers from coarse wool is the greatest gift of a holiday.
**You:** What special food do you eat on holidays?
**Ghost:** Warm honeyed buns come straight from the domed oven on feast mornings. The cook stirs bits of dried elderberry and sweet herbs into fine flour, sharing pieces with the yard folk. It tastes far richer than our usual spelt mash, making the morning feel truly blessed.
**You:** What do town folk do outside on holidays?
**Ghost:** Go safely through the streets. I must get back to my shuttle before the daylight fades from the yard.
