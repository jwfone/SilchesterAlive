# soldier walks

### Walk 1

**Ghost:** Greetings, friend. Marcus Valerius is my name. After twenty-five years of marching, I am happily settled here with my spade and plow. What news do you bring to my quiet gate?
**You:** Who are you and what did you do?
**Ghost:** You speak to Marcus Valerius, former legionary. Twenty-five years of service with the Second Legion taught me how to work hard and keep my boots dry. I earned my discharge pay and settled down right here outside the town boundary to grow crops and live as a quiet farmer.
**You:** What is happening in the town?
**Ghost:** Travelers say the province is peaceful now, which is good for farming. In town, they are clearing more trees to extend the straight gravel roads. Trade seems to be growing every month.
**You:** Any news from the army?
**Ghost:** My old comrades in the legion say they are busy building forts further north. They need steady supplies of grain and beer, which keeps our market prices quite healthy and stable.
**You:** What coins do you have?
**Ghost:** Silver denarii are what I prefer to keep in my leather pouch. I earned plenty of them during my service, and they still buy the best iron tools or fine salt. For small things like a cup of sour wine or some fresh greens, copper coins do the job just fine.
**You:** Is barley useful to sell around here?
**Ghost:** I plant acres of good barley every spring. It is a reliable grain that feeds my family, keeps our few beasts happy through winter, and brings in solid coin when sold to passing traders and local tavern keepers across the settlement.
**You:** How do you deal with the army quartermasters?
**Ghost:** Safe travels on those new gravel roads. Do come back soon.

### Walk 2

**Ghost:** Back again, are you? It is good to see a friendly face. Pull up a bench by the hearth. I was just checking on the barley crop before the sun goes down.
**You:** What is your house like?
**Ghost:** Welcome to my place. It is built entirely of timber, clay, and thatch, standing outside the town boundary. The central room holds our cooking fire, while the animal sheds sit right alongside to protect our livestock from wolves and stray dogs during the chilly British nights.
**You:** What do you do all day on the farm?
**Ghost:** My daily work starts before the birds even sing, checking our timber barn and feeding the animals. Tending the barley takes up most of my afternoon, ensuring the weeds do not choke the crop. It is hard physical labour, but it keeps a veteran fit and healthy.
**You:** Do the beasts take a lot of work each day?
**Ghost:** Those hungry pigs will wake the whole household if their trough is empty at sunrise. I carry heavy buckets of slops out to them first thing, then check the chickens for fresh eggs. Our cow needs milking too, which is gentle work for scarred old hands.
**You:** Do you have any farm animals?
**Ghost:** Two pigs, three dairy cows, and a flock of noisy chickens share my smallholding. The chickens give us fresh eggs, the cows provide milk, and the pigs are fattening up nicely on acorns from the nearby woods.
**You:** Tell me about the pigs' food.
**Ghost:** Oak woods nearby provide plenty of acorns, which makes excellent feed for my two pigs. It costs me nothing but a little time to watch them root. They grow fat and strong on what they find.
**You:** What are the cows for?
**Ghost:** Go well, friend. Keep your boots dry and your heart glad.

### Walk 3

**Ghost:** You return to my gate. Welcome. The dusty roads of Calleva are busy today, so my quiet yard is a fine place to rest your boots. What shall we speak of now?
**You:** What is the market like?
**Ghost:** Every market day, the open space in Calleva fills with noise and shouting. Farmers from the countryside bring their sheep, while traders set up simple wooden stalls. I carry my surplus barley there in sacks to sell to the town folk and visitors.
**You:** What do you eat every day?
**Ghost:** Coarse barley bread and pork salt-cured from last winter form the core of our meals. My wife boils up vegetable stews over the central fire with garlic and herbs. It is much like army rations, though having fresh eggs and milk makes it far tastier!
**You:** Where do you get your meat?
**Ghost:** Salt is the secret to keeping meat good throughout the summer. We buy coarse salt from traders at the market and rub it deep into slices of pork. Hung high in the smoke above our cooking hearth, it keeps safe from flies and stays tasty all year.
**You:** Is porridge your main meal?
**Ghost:** We crush grain between two quern stones, then boil it slow over the hearth fire in an iron pot. Adding a splash of goat milk or a spoonful of honey makes it sweet. It fills your stomach right up before you head out into cold morning mist.
**You:** Where is the fire inside your farmstead?
**Ghost:** Fire sits at the core of our daily life here. Situated safely away from the wooden walls, the open hearth boils our water and roasts salted meat. It gives us light during dark evenings, bringing the whole family together around the warmth after a hard day of farm work.
**You:** Tell me about the buildings in Calleva.
**Ghost:** Go well, friend. Keep your boots dry and your heart glad.

### Walk 6

**Ghost:** You return to my gate. Welcome. The dusty roads of Calleva are busy today, so my quiet yard is a fine place to rest your boots. What shall we speak of now?
**You:** Tell me about the streets in Calleva.
**Ghost:** Military surveyors are busy using brass instruments to lay out straight lines through the old woods. They are marking out proper square blocks, just like a legionary marching camp but made for peace. It is strange to see such neat corners in this wild countryside.
**You:** Where do you sell your barley?
**Ghost:** The market is a busy, muddy place near the middle of our new streets. You can hear sheep bleating and merchants shouting about their pottery from across the channel. I bring my grain there early in the morning to get the best spot.
**You:** How do you deal with the army quartermasters?
**Ghost:** I sell my extra barley and salted pork directly to army quartermasters. They need vast amounts of food for the troops and draft horses. They pay in solid silver denarii, which is far better than bartering at market when I need coin.
**You:** What was life like as a Roman soldier?
**Ghost:** Twenty-five years in Legio II Augusta taught me discipline and hard work. We marched across Britannia with heavy packs, cleared thick forests, and built sturdy timber forts. That military training makes clearing tree roots and digging farm ditches feel like second nature now.
**You:** Who are you and what did you do?
**Ghost:** They call me Marcus Valerius. For a quarter of a century I wore military boots and carried a shield with Legio II Augusta across Britannia. These days, I am just a retired veteran working a timber farmstead here in Calleva, trading my grain for honest coin instead of chasing battle.
**You:** How did you end up living here in Calleva?
**Ghost:** May the gods keep your path smooth and your pack light. Farewell.
