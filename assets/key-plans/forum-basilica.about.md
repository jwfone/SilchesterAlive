# The forum and basilica: how this reconstruction was made

Nothing of Calleva's forum and basilica stands above ground today; the site was excavated, recorded and filled in
again. What you see is a reconstruction. The plan is surveyed fact: every wall sits on the Society of Antiquaries'
"Great Plan", as digitised by the Silchester Mapping Project. Most of what rises above about a metre is an informed
reconstruction. The colours in the **Evidence** view show which is which.

## The date shown

The forum and basilica were the centre of the town and of the civitas of the Atrebates: a market place, and the
seat of administration and justice. The masonry building was put up in the mid-2nd century (under Hadrian, finished
by about AD 150), replacing a timber forum and basilica of the late 1st century. This model shows it **around
AD 270–280**, the same date as the rest of the town.

By then the great hall had a new use. Excavation found **iron-smithing, and some bronze-working, in the basilica
from the mid-3rd century to the end of the Roman period**. So the hall is shown with an earth floor and a few
smithing hearths, while the forum's shops, offices and porticos stay in use. Whether the town's government still met
here, perhaps upstairs or in the forum, is not known.

## How the plan was made

- **1730s–1870s:** John Stair, then the Revd J. G. Joyce, dug the site; Joyce uncovered the whole building.
- **1892:** the Society of Antiquaries re-planned it and described it in detail (Fox and St John Hope, 1893).
- **1977, 1980–86:** Michael Fulford's excavation of the basilica found the earlier timber buildings beneath it,
  dated the masonry building, and found the metalworking.
- **GIS:** the Great Plan rings are the walls in this model. A photograph of the plan in Fulford's guide (2002)
  was laid over them to read what each wall was.
- The whole complex is turned about 2.5° from the street grid, and the main entrance is about 1.5 m south of the
  middle of the east side, to face the street from the east gate.

## Walking through the building

1. **East front:** an outer colonnade runs right round the building. The main entrance is a gateway on a
   foundation 49 ft (15 m) long; it carried an inscription in letters 28 cm high.
2. **Vestibule and inner gateway:** a passage through the east range to a second gateway in the line of the
   courtyard colonnade. A great drain ran out beneath them.
3. **The forum court:** an open gravelled square about 40 × 45 m, with a colonnade on three sides.
4. **The ranges:** shops on the north and east, opening on both porticos; on the south, offices or courts,
   two of them with apses; on the north, an **exedra**, an apse open to the portico, perhaps for a statue.
5. **The basilica:** a hall 82 m long (with its end apses) and 17.7 m wide inside, its roof carried partly on
   **one row of Corinthian columns** about 8.6 m high. At each end a raised **tribunal** in an apse, where
   magistrates may have sat to give judgement.
6. **The west range:** offices, and in the middle an apsidal room open to the hall up three steps, lined with
   marble: perhaps the council chamber (curia) or a shrine of the civitas.

## What is evidence and what is reconstruction

**Green: found at Silchester.** The wall lines and room plan; walls of **flint with bonding courses of tile**
and brick quoins; one row of hall columns on a sleeper wall 4 ft 6 in wide; Corinthian capitals and drums 2 ft 10 in
across; the courtyard columns (shafts about 0.57 m across, 4.4 m apart, at least 4.7 m high, with timber beams over
them); raised floors in the tribunals and the curia; marble linings, tile and mosaic floors and painted plaster in the
apses; the gateway foundations; the drain; the metalworking.

**Amber: by comparison.** The courtyard and outer portico roofs and the forum ranges' height and gable roofs
(as in other Roman forums and reconstructions); the outer columns, assumed a little smaller than the courtyard
ones; the gateway columns, sized from a wall-attached column base found loose in the forum; Vitruvius's rule that a
basilica's aisle is a third of its nave, which the Silchester hall matches.

**Red: conjecture.** The basilica's height (eaves 15 m), clerestory windows and roofs; the spacing of the hall
columns; the gateway superstructures; most doorways; windows in the ranges; the smithing hearths' number and places.

## Choices worth knowing about

- **One colonnade, not two.** The Victorians drew a second row of columns in the hall by symmetry; Fulford's
  excavation found only one, so only one is shown.
- **The tribunals are open apses.** The Victorians thought the apses were later squared off; they are shown as
  apses, open to the hall, as on Fulford's plan.
- **The flint walls** follow the town wall, built about the same time: about five courses of flint, then a double
  course of tile.
- **The gateways** are sized from the evidence rather than to look grand: about 9 m (outer) and 7.5 m (inner).
- The Victorians proposed **flat roofs** over the inner porticos to light the rooms behind; pitched roofs with
  windows above them are shown instead, as in modern reconstruction paintings.
