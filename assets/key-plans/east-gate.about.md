# The east gate: how this reconstruction was made

Only foundations of Calleva's gates were found; the wall still stands round the town, but nowhere to its full height. What you see is a reconstruction: the plan is surveyed fact, almost everything above the surviving wall is informed conjecture. The colours in the **Evidence** view show which is which.

The main gate, on the road east to London. Joyce found it in 1872, at a slant to the street grid; the Antiquaries
dug it again in 1908. Only its foundations survived.

## What you see

- **A double gateway with guardrooms**, like the west gate "but fractionally more substantial": two passages about 4.9 m long
  with arches 3.7 m wide.
- **The guardhouses** are shown with two rooms each, as at the west gate (only the outline is known here), rising as towers above
  the wall-walk (conjecture).
- The wall-walk reaches each tower over a small landing on its field side (conjecture).
- How the ditch was crossed is not known; a causeway is shown.

## The town wall

Calleva's stone wall was built **about AD 260–280**, in front of an earlier earth rampart and ditch of around AD 200. Its builders
cut back the face of the old bank, built the wall about 2 m in front of it and filled the gap with clay; the old V-shaped ditch was
filled in and a broad, shallow ditch dug further out. The wall runs 2.4 km round the town. It is about **3 m thick** at the base,
stepped back 0.6 m on the inside about 2 m up; at intervals of about 60 m the full thickness is carried up as a broad pilaster
(a *counterfort*), perhaps where stairs led up to the walk. It is built of **flint**, quarried from the chalk at least 10 km
away, with a **double course of flat stone slabs every four or five courses of flint**: limestone, some of it from the Bath
area, and brown ironstone.

What survives stands up to 4.5 m high near the south gate, and has lost almost all its outer face.

## What is evidence and what is reconstruction

**Green: found at Silchester.** The line of the wall (the Silchester Mapping Project's digitisation, from the Ordnance Survey and
the excavation plans); its thickness, inner step, plinth and counterforts (17 of them where the survey shows them); the flint
and slab facing; the gate plans (from the excavations of the 1870s and 1890s); the rampart behind the wall and the ditch in front.

**Amber: by comparison.** The crenellated parapet: no Silchester evidence either way, but usual on Roman walls.

**Red: conjecture.** Every height above what survives: the wall-walk is placed at 4.2 m and the merlons at 6 m (a reading of
George Boon's estimate of "6 m high with a parapet of 1.8 m"); everything over the gates; the steps up the counterforts; where 22
of the counterforts stand (spaced about 60 m apart, as the excavators described); the bridges over the ditch; the size of the
rampart and ditch, taken from the excavated sections.
