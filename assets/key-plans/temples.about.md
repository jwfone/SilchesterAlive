# The temples: how this reconstruction was made

Nothing of these buildings stands above ground today. They lie under the field west of St Mary's church, the churchyard and the
farmyard of the Old Manor House. Grave-diggers struck them in 1890, and the Society of Antiquaries traced what they could outside
the churchyard. What you see is a reconstruction. The plan is surveyed fact: every wall sits on the Society of Antiquaries' "Great
Plan", as digitised by the Silchester Mapping Project. Everything above the floors is an informed reconstruction. The colours in the
**Evidence** view show which is which.

## What the buildings were

Two **Romano-Celtic temples** in a walled sacred precinct (*temenos*) near the east gate, where the road from London entered the town.
A temple of this British and Gaulish kind is a square shrine (the *cella*), which held the god's image, inside a square walkway (the
*ambulatory*). Worship happened mostly outside, in the precinct. The larger temple, 22 m square, is still the largest of its kind known
in Roman Britain. Which gods were worshipped here is not known: probably Celtic gods, perhaps paired with Roman ones (Fulford).

A third temple stood to the north. The University of Reading dug half of it in 2017 and found it was the earliest stone building yet known
at Silchester, begun under Nero. It seems to have been pulled down around AD 200, so it is not shown.

## The date shown

The temples are not dated, but their neighbour was built in the AD 60s or 70s. If the three were built together, as their symmetry suggests,
the precinct is one of the town's oldest monuments. It is shown **around AD 270–280**, the same date as the rest of the town, with both
temples standing.

## Walking through the precinct

1. **The precinct wall** runs along the west side for 110 m and turns at both ends. Its north end points at the east gate. No gateway was
   found in it, and its east side lies under the churchyard and was never found, so the precinct is shown open on the east.
2. **The large temple** stands on a solid platform 2.3 m high, its floors laid on earth packed between the walls. Climb the steps on the
   east and walk round the cella under the colonnade. Its floor was hard pink concrete; the cella's was polished, set with chips of dark
   Purbeck marble.
3. **The small temple** has a lower platform. Its walkway is closed in by walls, lit by small windows; a door on the east leads in and a
   second door into the cella.
4. Both temples were **plastered outside and painted bright red**. Fragments of it survived on their walls.
5. **Inside each cella** stands the god's statue on a pedestal, facing the door, with an offering table before it. Two small clay lamps
   were found at the temples; here they stand on the table. The walls are lined with dark Purbeck marble to waist height, with white
   stucco panels above, and inscribed marble plaques record gifts to the god.
6. **Before each temple's steps stands an altar.** Sacrifice and most worship took place out here in the open, not inside the cella.

## What is evidence and what is reconstruction

**Green: found at Silchester.** The wall lines, sizes and thicknesses; the large temple's floor height and its two floors; the earth fill
of both platforms; the red-painted plaster outside; flint walls with tile courses in the precinct wall.

**Amber: by comparison.** Column proportions (Vitruvius's rule of seven diameters) and timber beams; tiled roofs at a low pitch; glazed
windows.

**Red: conjecture.** Every height above the floors; the colonnade (no column was found); the small temple's floor height; the roofs'
form; all doors, steps and windows; the floors of the small temple; the offering tables.

The statues, plaques and altars are **amber**: nothing of them was found in these temples, but each follows something found elsewhere (see
below). The marble and stucco wall linings are **green**: the materials were found here, though where they were on the walls is a guess.

Note that the wall colours follow the wall plan, so the walls show green even where their height is conjecture.

## Choices worth knowing about

- **The east halves are restored.** Only the western parts could be dug in 1890; the rest lies under the churchyard and was completed on
  the plan "from analogy". The large temple's north-east corner was found, in a sawpit.
- **A colonnade on the large temple, walls on the small one.** The excavators thought the large temple's thick outer wall could have carried
  columns all round, and that the small temple's thinner wall could not. Many Romano-Celtic temples had a walled walkway instead.
- **The columns** are given the size of the forum's colonnade, the one set measured at Silchester. None was found here.
- **The cella towers** rise just high enough for windows above the walkway roofs: the only way their shrines could have been lit. The walls
  were not thick enough to carry a very tall tower.
- **Doors and steps on the east.** None was found; the east side lies under the churchyard. The northern temple faced east, onto the road
  from London, and so do most temples of this type.
- **Inside**, no statue, base or altar was found in either temple, and nobody knows which god lived here. So the statues are plain
  robed figures holding an offering dish, with nothing to name the god. Their size and stone follow the cult statue of Mercury found at
  Uley in Gloucestershire: limestone, a little over life size, on a base. The excavators did find thin slabs of Purbeck marble "evidently"
  used as wall linings, and white stucco panel mouldings; these line the cellae here. The plaques follow the thin Purbeck marble
  dedications found inside the shrine of another Silchester temple (in Insula XXXV), gifts from a guild of foreigners living at Calleva.
  Their words here are made up and deliberately unreadable.
- **The altars** stand in the open before the steps, as the Roman architect Vitruvius advised: facing east, and lower than the statue.
  They are of greensand, like the half-carved altar found buried in this precinct in 2017; the small temple's is that block's size.
- **St Mary's church** stands on the precinct's east side. Its axis is exactly parallel with the small temple's, which may be chance, or may
  mean the church was built on an older building of the precinct.
