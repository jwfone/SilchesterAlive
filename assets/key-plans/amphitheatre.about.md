# The amphitheatre: how this reconstruction was made

The earth banks of the amphitheatre still stand outside the town's east wall, and the lower courses of its stone arena wall can
be seen. It was excavated by Professor Michael Fulford of the University of Reading in 1979–85. The plan you walk on is
surveyed fact: the arena wall, the two entrance passages and the two small recesses follow the Silchester Mapping Project's
plan of the stone amphitheatre, and the banks follow the surviving earthwork. Everything above the lowest courses, and all the
timber, is an informed reconstruction. The colours in the **Evidence** view show which is which.

## What the building was

An amphitheatre for shows: gladiators, wild beasts and other blood sports, and public executions, were the usual fare in the
Roman world, though nothing found here says which took place at Calleva (horse bones suggest that horses took part). It was
first built of earth and timber between about AD 55 and 75, when the arena was dug out about 2 m into a natural hollow and the
soil heaped up into banks for the seats. It held perhaps 4,500 to 9,000 people.

## The date shown

The amphitheatre is shown **around AD 270–280**, the same date as the rest of the town. By then its arena had been rebuilt
twice: made oval in the 2nd century, and walled in stone, with stone entrances and recesses, in the first half of the 3rd
century. Its floors were raised with sand and gravel a little later, perhaps in the 250s. It fell out of use in the 4th century.

## Walking through the building

1. **The entrances:** two passages on the long, north–south axis, walled in stone. The north one is about 5.3 m wide, the south
   one about 3.8 m: the excavators found them of different widths. Each rises from the arena floor to the old ground level.
2. **The arena:** about 45 by 39 m inside its wall, its floor of sand and gravel some 2 m below the ground outside.
3. **The arena wall:** of flint and brown ironstone, about 3 m high, holding back the banks and carrying the seating.
4. **The recesses:** two small semicircular rooms off the arena on the east–west axis, probably vaulted. No one knows what
   they were for: elsewhere such recesses held altars to Nemesis (fate); they may also have been refuges for the performers.
5. **The seats:** rows of timber benches on the earth banks, rising from the top of the arena wall. At the top is a walk with a
   fence, and the bank falls away outside.

## What is evidence and what is reconstruction

**Green: found at Silchester.** The line, thickness and plan of the arena wall, the entrance passages and the recesses; the
arena dug 2 m below the ground; the sand and gravel floors; the earth banks and how far they spread; the absence of an outer
wall; the walls' flint with single courses of brown ironstone, as they survive in the recesses today.

**Amber: by comparison or the excavator's reconstruction.** The timber seating (twelve rows, as on the excavator's drawing of
the stone phase); the fence along the top; the vaults over the recesses.

**Red: conjecture.** The timber rail on the arena wall; the outside stairs (the first, timber, amphitheatre had stairs up the
outside of the banks, but where they stood is not known); the arched heads of the recess doorways; the wall tops.

## Choices worth knowing about

- **The heights follow the excavator's reconstruction** of the stone phase: a wall about 3 m high, twelve rows of seats rising
  a further 4.3 m. Today's banks stand 4–5 m above the field round them, close to this.
- **The walls are flint with single courses of flat brown ironstone**, at uneven heights, as in the recess walls that stand
  today. Unlike the town wall and the forum, there are no courses of tile.
- **No gates** are shown in the passages: gates were set in the 2nd-century timber amphitheatre, but none is recorded for the
  stone one.
- **Not shown:** a raised platform (tribunal) over a recess, which the excavators thought possible; the medieval hall later built
  in the arena.
